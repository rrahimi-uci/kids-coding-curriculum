\section{Lesson questions}
\begin{answer}[Exit question]
The if is outside the forever loop, so it checks only once, right when the flag is clicked. At that moment the ghost is not touching the Ballerina, so it skips the say block and never asks again. Inside the forever, it asks again and again.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Reflection]
Real-life ifs: if it rains, take an umbrella; if the light is red, stop; if you are hungry, eat a snack. Any drawing of something a sprite touched is right: the bowl, the mouse-pointer, the edge, the Ballerina.
\end{answer}

\section{Workbook exercises}
\begin{answer}[Exercise 2.1: Boo Ghost]
\textbf{What you should see:} after the green flag, nothing happens until the mouse-pointer touches the ghost. Then the ghost says \enquote{Boo!} for 1 second. If the mouse stays on the ghost, it says \enquote{Boo!} again and again. Moving the mouse away stops it.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} the if is inside the forever \quad \tickbox{} \enquote{touching mouse-pointer?} sits in the pointy hole \quad \tickbox{} the say block is inside the if.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 2.2: Catch the Apple]
\textbf{What you should see:} after the green flag, the Star disappears. The apple jumps to where the Star was and falls slowly, the right way up. The right and left arrow keys move the bowl along the bottom. If the bowl catches the apple, a pop plays and the apple starts again from the Star. If the apple reaches the bottom edge, it starts again from the Star with no sound. This goes on until the stop sign.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} the Star hides \quad \tickbox{} both ifs are inside the apple's forever \quad \tickbox{} the touching menus say Bowl and edge \quad \tickbox{} the bowl stays the right way up when it goes left \quad \tickbox{} the game is saved for week 3.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 2.3: Ghost Tag]
\textbf{Circle:} the \enquote{if touching Ballerina? then} block, above the forever. It checks once at the start, when the ghost is far away, and never again.

\textbf{Fix:} drag the if (the say block comes with it) into the forever, under \enquote{if on edge, bounce}.

\textbf{What you should see after the fix:} the ghost flies from side to side. Each time it reaches the Ballerina, it stops and says \enquote{Got you!} for 1 second, then flies on. It may say it two or three times as it passes through her: that is fine.
\end{answer}

\section{Play-at-home card}
\begin{answer}[Simon says IF]
The child does the action only when the rule is true for them, and stays still when it is not. In the forever rule, they clap every time the spoon goes up, not only the first time.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Two stars and a wish]
Two specific things the child liked (\enquote{I like the red roof}) and one kind, useful wish (\enquote{I wish it had a door}). Help them make the wish about the work, not the person.
\end{answer}
