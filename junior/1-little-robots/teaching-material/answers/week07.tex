\section{Lesson questions}
\begin{answer}[Exit question]
Add \enquote{wait 2 seconds} (orange Control) at the top of the Ghost's script, right under its green flag block. The Ghost talks second, so the Ghost waits while the cat talks.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Reflection]
\enquote{Who made the cat picture?} The Scratch team (the people who make Scratch). \enquote{What does wait do?} It makes the sprite stop for a little while before the next block, so the other sprite can have its turn. Any drawing of two sprites talking is right.
\end{answer}

\section{Workbook exercises}
\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.1: Hello, Ballerina!]
\textbf{What you should see:} the backdrop the child picked, with the cat and the Ballerina on it. After the green flag, the cat says \enquote{Hi, Ballerina!} for 2 seconds. When the cat's bubble goes away, the Ballerina says \enquote{Hi, Cat!} for 2 seconds.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} each sprite has its own green-flag script (click each sprite in the sprite list to check) \quad \tickbox{} the wait is at the top of the Ballerina's script \quad \tickbox{} a backdrop is chosen.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.2: Boo at Once]
The arrow points to the Ghost's script, between the green flag block and \enquote{say Boo!}. That is where the wait goes. With the bug, both bubbles appear at the same time. After adding \enquote{wait 2 seconds} there, the cat says \enquote{Who is there?} for 2 seconds, then the Ghost says \enquote{Boo!}.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.3: My Two-Line Story]
Any plan with two sprites and two lines is right: box 1 shows who talks first and what they say, box 2 shows who answers. The words can be drawn, said out loud or written by a grown-up.

\textbf{What you should see:} a backdrop the child picked, two sprites from the library (for example Cat, Ballerina, Ghost or Star), and after the green flag the first line, then the second line, one after the other. The project matches the plan.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} the project matches the two boxes \quad \tickbox{} the second sprite's script starts with a wait \quad \tickbox{} nicknames only \quad \tickbox{} the child gives credit: \enquote{The sprites and backdrop were made by the Scratch team.}
\end{answer}

\section{Play-at-home card}
\begin{answer}[Knock, knock!]
Taking turns makes the joke work. When both talk at once, nobody can hear the joke, like two sprites with no wait.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Credit detective]
The cover (or the first page) names the author, who wrote the words, and the illustrator, who drew the pictures. Any book where the child finds a name is right.
\end{answer}
