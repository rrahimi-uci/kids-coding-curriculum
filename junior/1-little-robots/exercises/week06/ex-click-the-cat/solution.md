# Teacher reference: Exercise 6.1 - Click the Cat
> TEACHER ONLY. Keep this file away from students. It is not published on the website.

**Level and week:** Junior Level 1, Week 6 (When I Click...)  **Kind:** Scratch (offline app)  **Time:** about 6 minutes
**Big idea:** An event starts a script. "when this sprite clicked" waits for a click on its own sprite; no green flag needed.

## The finished project
Setup: File > New. Keep the cat.

Sprite: Sprite1 (the cat)
- when this sprite clicked
- play sound Meow until done
- say That tickles! for 2 seconds

## What you should see
- Before any click: nothing happens, even if the flag is clicked.
- After a click on the cat: a meow, then a speech bubble "That tickles!" for 2 seconds.
- It works again on every click.

## Talking points
- The clap game: the cat's script waits for its own event, a click on the cat.
- The green flag is one event; a click is another. Both blocks are yellow and have a round hat top.
- Ask: "What does this hat block wait for?"

## Common mistakes and quick fixes
- The child clicks the green flag and waits: point at the hat block and ask what it waits for.
- The child drags the cat instead of clicking it: a quick click runs the script; holding and moving drags.
- "when green flag clicked" was used instead: swap it for "when this sprite clicked" (drag the old hat away, snap the new one on top).
- A real name in the speech bubble: change it to a nickname.

## Check-off list
- [ ] The script starts with "when this sprite clicked": the yellow hat block is on top.
- [ ] All three blocks are snapped together: dragging the top block moves the whole script.
- [ ] Clicking the cat (not the flag) makes it meow and talk: watch one run.
