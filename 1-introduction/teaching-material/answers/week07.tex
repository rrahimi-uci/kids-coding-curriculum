\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.1: Hello, Python!]
\pyfile{../exercises/week07/ex-hello-python/solution.py}
What to look for: four \textbf{different} \texttt{print} lines, the last containing \enquote{bye}; a nickname, not a full name. Any messages are correct. Bonus: a comment of their own.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.2: My First Square]
\pyfile{../exercises/week07/ex-first-square/solution.py}
What to look for: four \texttt{forward}/\texttt{left} pairs with the same distance. A square drawn with \texttt{right(90)} turns, or a different side length, is also correct.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 7.3: Typo Hunt]
\pyfile{../exercises/week07/ex-typo-hunt/solution.py}
The four typos, in the order Python finds them: line 12 \texttt{Print} → \texttt{print} (\texttt{NameError}); line 16 \texttt{forwad} → \texttt{forward} (\texttt{AttributeError}); line 21 \texttt{Right} → \texttt{right}; line 26 \texttt{Done} → \texttt{done}. What to look for: the student fixed them one at a time and can say which line each error pointed to.
\end{answer}

\section{Homework}
\begin{answer}[Core: Triangle Time]
\pyfile{../exercises/week07/hw-triangle/solution.py}
Paper versions: accept any program with three \texttt{forward(120)}/\texttt{left(120)} pairs. A common mistake is \texttt{left(60)} (the inside angle): the turtle then draws half a hexagon. Ask: \enquote{How far does the turtle turn at a corner?}
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Challenge: Be the turtle]
A line 100 to the right, then 50 up, then 100 back to the left: three sides of a rectangle, open on the left, like a backwards C (or a squared letter U lying on its side). The turtle ends above the starting dot.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Creative: My initial]
Any letter drawn with straight lines. Look for steps written as F/L/R with numbers, and a program that matches the plan. Example for L (starting at the top, facing down): \texttt{t.right(90)}, \texttt{t.forward(100)}, \texttt{t.left(90)}, \texttt{t.forward(60)}.
\end{answer}
