\section{Lesson questions}
\begin{answer}[Exit question]
No. The script is in the cat's code, so it waits for a click on the cat. Clicking the Ballerina does not start it.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Reflection]
Three ways to start a script: the green flag, clicking a sprite, and pressing a key. Any event the child circles is right; ask why they like it. Any key they name that matches their script is right.
\end{answer}

\section{Workbook exercises}
\begin{answer}[Exercise 6.1: Click the Cat]
\textbf{What you should see and hear:} nothing happens until the cat is clicked. Then the cat meows, and after the meow it says \enquote{That tickles!} in a speech bubble for 2 seconds. It works every time the cat is clicked, without the green flag.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} the script starts with \enquote{when this sprite clicked} \quad \tickbox{} all blocks clicked together \quad \tickbox{} the child clicks the cat, not the flag.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 6.2: Keyboard Cat]
\textbf{What you should see:} each press of the right arrow key moves the cat a little to the right and changes its costume, so it looks as if it walks. Holding the key down makes it keep walking. Each press of the space bar makes the cat spin once all the way round (four quarter turns) and stop facing the same way as before.

\textbf{What to look for:} \tickbox{} two separate scripts, each with its own yellow hat block \quad \tickbox{} the dropdowns say \enquote{right arrow} and \enquote{space} \quad \tickbox{} the turn block is inside the repeat. If keys do nothing, click the stage once and try again.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 6.3: The Mixed-Up Ballerina]
\textbf{Bug 1, wrong sprite:} circle the cat's script. \enquote{when this sprite clicked, say Hi!} is in the cat's code, so clicking the cat makes the cat say \enquote{Hi!} and clicking the Ballerina does nothing. Fix: drag the script onto the Ballerina in the sprite list (this copies it), then delete it from the cat.

\textbf{Bug 2, wrong key:} circle the \enquote{a} in \enquote{when a key pressed}. Kai's plan says space. Fix: pick space in the dropdown.

\textbf{What you should see after the fix:} clicking the Ballerina makes her say \enquote{Hi!} for 2 seconds. Pressing space makes her change costume four times, one each second, so she dances. Clicking the cat does nothing.
\end{answer}

\section{Play-at-home card}
\begin{answer}[When I clap, you jump]
The child does the right action for each event and nothing for an event that has no card. For \enquote{banana}, nothing happens because no card listens for it, like a script that waits for a different key.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[A nickname for my sprite]
Any made-up nickname is right. It should not be the child's real name. In Scratch, look for \enquote{when this sprite clicked} and a say block with the nickname.
\end{answer}
