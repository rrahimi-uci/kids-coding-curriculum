# Teacher reference: Exercise 7.3 - My Two-Line Story
> TEACHER ONLY. Keep this file away from students. It is not published on the website.

**Level and week:** Junior Level 1, Week 7 (Two Sprites Talk)  **Kind:** Scratch (offline app), plan and build  **Time:** about 8 minutes (2 to plan, 6 to build)
**Big idea:** Plan first, then build one box at a time. A tiny story plan becomes two scripts, one per sprite.

## The finished project
Setup: File > New. Keep the cat (or delete it and pick two others). Add a second sprite from the library (Ballerina, Ghost or Star) and any backdrop. This is one example; any two-line story that matches the child's plan is right.

Plan box 1: the Star says "I am lost!"  Plan box 2: the Ghost says "Follow me!"

Sprite: Star
- when green flag clicked
- say I am lost! for 2 seconds

Sprite: Ghost
- when green flag clicked
- wait 2 seconds
- say Follow me! for 2 seconds

Optional credit comment (right-click the code area, "Add Comment", typed by the grown-up): Sprites and backdrop: Scratch team. Story: Zoe.

## What you should see
- The chosen backdrop and two sprites in their own spots.
- After the green flag: line 1 over the first sprite for 2 seconds, then line 2 over the second sprite for 2 seconds.
- The project matches the two boxes in the workbook.

## Talking points
- Ask "Does it match your plan?" This is a first taste of the week 9 storyboard.
- Ask "Who made the pictures? Who made the story?" (The Scratch team made the sprites and backdrop; the child made the story.)
- Nicknames only in the lines and in the credit comment.

## Common mistakes and quick fixes
- The plan grows to many lines: praise it, and build the first two lines first. More lines are the extension (say, wait 2, say).
- Both scripts in one sprite: click each sprite in the sprite list to check.
- The wait is missing or in the first speaker's script: see Exercise 7.2.
- The child types a long line: keep lines to a few words; the grown-up can help type.

## Check-off list
- [ ] The plan has two boxes: who talks first, who answers.
- [ ] Two sprites, each with its own green-flag script; the second one starts with a wait.
- [ ] A backdrop is chosen and the story matches the plan: watch one run.
- [ ] The child gives credit out loud (or in a comment).
