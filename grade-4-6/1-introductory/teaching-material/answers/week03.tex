\section{Lesson and workbook questions}
\begin{answer}[Exit ticket]
The if checks only once, right after the flag is clicked, when the cat is not at the edge. Put the if inside a forever loop so it keeps checking.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Reflection: \enquote{when key pressed} vs \enquote{if touching}]
\enquote{When space key pressed} is an event: it waits and starts its script when the key is pressed. \enquote{If touching\ldots} is a question: it is asked only when the script reaches it, so it needs a loop to keep asking. Accept any answer that says one \enquote{waits for something to happen} and the other \enquote{checks / asks a question}.
\end{answer}

\section{Workbook exercises}
\begin{answer}[Exercise 3.1: Arrow-Key Explorer]
\textbf{Expected behavior:} after clicking the stage once, the right arrow moves the sprite 10 steps right, left arrow 10 left, up arrow 10 up, down arrow 10 down. Holding a key moves it repeatedly. There is no green-flag script, and none is needed: each key is its own event.

\textbf{Question:} the \textbf{up arrow} makes y bigger.

\textbf{Scoring:} \tickbox{} four separate hat blocks \quad \tickbox{} correct key in each dropdown \quad \tickbox{} correct signs ($+10$ / $-10$).
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 3.2: Tickle the Ghost]
\textbf{Expected behavior:} after the flag, whenever the mouse pointer is over the ghost it grows to 150\,\% and says \enquote{Boo! That tickles!}; when the mouse moves away it shrinks back to 100\,\% and the bubble disappears. This keeps working until the stop sign is clicked.

\textbf{Prediction without forever:} nothing happens when the mouse touches the ghost, because the if-else is checked only once, right after the flag (when the mouse is on the flag button, not on the ghost). The ghost just stays normal size.

\textbf{What to look for:} the if-else is inside forever; the empty \enquote{say} in the else part (without it, the \enquote{Boo!} bubble stays on screen).
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Exercise 3.3: Treasure Hunt]
\textbf{Star's script:} the star appears in the top-right corner (180, 120) and spins forever.

\textbf{Sam's script, what happens:} the explorer goes to the bottom-left start; steering it onto the star does nothing, no message.
\textbf{Why:} the if is checked only once, straight after the flag is clicked, when the explorer is far from the star. Then the script ends.
\textbf{Fix:} put the if (with its two blocks inside) into a forever loop, under \enquote{go to x: $-180$ y: $-120$}:
\begin{center}
\begin{scratch}[scale=0.8]
\blockinit{when \greenflag clicked}
\blockmove{go to x: \ovalnum{-180} y: \ovalnum{-120}}
\blockinfloop{forever}{
  \blockif{if \boolsensing{touching \ovalsensing*{Star} ?} then}{
    \blocklook{say \ovalnum{Treasure found!} for \ovalnum{2} seconds}
    \blockmove{go to x: \ovalnum{-180} y: \ovalnum{-120}}
  }
}
\end{scratch}
\end{center}
\textbf{Expected behavior after the fix:} each time the explorer touches the star it says \enquote{Treasure found!} for 2 seconds and then jumps back to the start, ready to play again. (The arrow-key scripts from 3.1 must be on the same sprite.)
\end{answer}

\section{Homework}
\begin{answer}[Core: Pet Reactions]
\textbf{Expected behavior:} clicking the pet plays a sound and changes its color (a new color on every click); pressing space switches to its next costume; after the green flag, whenever the mouse is over the pet it says \enquote{Pet me more!}, and the bubble disappears when the mouse moves away.

\textbf{Scoring:} three different events used; if-else inside forever. Any animal and any sound are fine. If a student swaps the color effect for a \enquote{say} block, its words never show: the pet is under the mouse when clicked, so the forever script replaces the bubble at once (a sprite has one bubble). That is a good discussion, not a fail.
\end{answer}

\begin{answer}[Challenge: Kai's bug]
The if runs only once, right after the green flag, when the mouse is not touching the pet. Fix: put the if inside a forever loop so it keeps checking.
\end{answer}

\end{answer}
