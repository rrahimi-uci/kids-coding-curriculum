\namedateline

\begin{core}[Growth Table (about 10 minutes)]
\textbf{1. Predict first.} How many steps does each search need in the worst case? Fill in the empty boxes \emph{before} you run any code.
Tips: linear search may have to look at every item. For binary search, use the halving ladder from workbook chapter 4: halve the number again and again (round down) and count the numbers until you reach 1. For 1\,000\,000, you can halve it, or use the pattern: every \enquote{times 10} adds about 3 steps. Guesses are fine: the code will tell you the real answer.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{|r|p{3cm}|p{3cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Items} & \textbf{Linear} & \textbf{Binary} \\ \hline
10 & & \\ \hline
100 & & \\ \hline
1000 & & \\ \hline
1\,000\,000 & & \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{2. Code.} Open \texttt{week04/hw-growth-table/starter.py}. Finish \texttt{linear\_worst(n)} and \texttt{halving\_steps(n)}: keep doing \texttt{n = n // 2} and count, until \texttt{n} is 0. (On paper, 10 goes 10, 5, 2, 1, 0: that is 4 halvings.)\\
\textbf{3. Check.} Run it and compare with your predictions. The \texttt{README.md} in the folder shows the table you should see. Then open \texttt{check\_my\_work.py} and press Run. Save a copy as \texttt{growth\_table.py} in \texttt{git-quest} (\menu{File > Save as...}), then \texttt{git add} and \texttt{git commit} it.
\end{core}

\begin{creative}[Draw the race]
