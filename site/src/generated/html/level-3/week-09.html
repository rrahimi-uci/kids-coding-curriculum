Rules or examples?

A program with rules does exactly what you typed: if teeth > 5: print("grumpy"). A learning program gets lots of examples with the right answers (labels) and finds the pattern itself. The things it measures are called features.

Our monsters have two features: fluff (0–10) and teeth (0–10). Put every example on a chart. To guess a mystery monster, find the closest example and copy its label. That is a nearest-neighbor classifier.

The mystery at (5, 2) is closest to a friendly monster, so the guess is friendly. Distance is measured with Pythagoras: math.sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy).

Is it any good? Train, then test

Split the examples into two piles. The program learns from the training pile. The test pile stays secret until the end; then we count how many test cards it labels correctly. accuracy=correct guessesnumber of test cards\text{accuracy} = \frac{\text{correct guesses}}{\text{number of test cards}} Testing on cards it learned from would be like seeing the exam answers first.

Bug huntBad examples, bad predictions

If the training pile has 8 grumpy monsters and only 1 friendly one, the program calls most friendly monsters grumpy. The code is fine – the data is the bug. This is called bias. When real AI decides things about people (faces, voices, job applications), biased examples can treat whole groups unfairly. That is why people must check the data, not just the code.

A tiny text generator

A Markov chain learns just one thing from a text: after each word, which words came next?

To write, it starts with a word and keeps picking a random next word from the list. The sentences sound like the training text, but the machine does not understand them at all. Today’s chatbots are enormously bigger and cleverer, but they also predict likely next words – which is why they can sound sure and still be wrong.

Big idea What it means Today
1 Perception Computers sense the world with cameras, microphones, sensors. a camera measuring fluff?
2 Representation & reasoning The world is stored as data the computer can reason about. each monster = (fluff, teeth)
3 Learning Computers find patterns in examples. nearest-neighbor classifier
4 Natural interaction Talking and writing like people is very hard. Markov story machine

SafetyUsing AI wisely and safely

AI can be wrong, even when it sounds sure – check important facts with a trusted source or adult. Never share personal information with a chatbot: no real name, address, school, passwords, photos or secrets; what you type may be stored. Deepfakes are fake pictures, voices or videos made by AI: ask “Who made this, and why?” before you believe or share, and never make fakes of real people. Bias: AI is only as fair as its examples.
