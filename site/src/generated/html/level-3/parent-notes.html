Dear families,

Welcome to Level 3: Advanced, the last level of our home coding course. This letter is for every family in the group, including friends who join us from another home. Please keep it handy: the last page has a question to ask your child each week.

Over 10–12 weekly sessions of 60 minutes, the students will learn to work like real software makers: saving their work with git, designing programs with classes and objects, using famous algorithms for searching, sorting and finding paths through mazes, exploring data, and building two small AI programs to see how machines learn from examples, and how they can get things wrong. The level ends with a final project of their own choice — a grid game, a generative art app or a data/AI app — and a showcase you are warmly invited to.

Each week there is a short homework set (15–20 minutes) that never needs the internet.

Our privacy promises

SafetyWhat we promise, and why

  • No accounts, no internet, no uploads. Everything runs on our own Macs with free tools (Thonny, Python and Apple’s git). Nothing your child makes is put online.

  • Nicknames only. Git writes an author name and an email into every save point. We use a nickname and a made-up placeholder email that ends in @example.com (a web address reserved for examples, which never receives mail). Your child’s real name, email address or school never goes into their projects.

  • No personal data in programs. Programs ask for a nickname or a favorite color, never a real name, age, address, school, photo or password. Survey data we analyze in week 8 is anonymous and collected only with permission.

  • AI chatbots are not needed. No part of this course uses an AI chatbot. In week 9 we talk about using them safely (see below).

  • Showcase photos only with permission. At the week 12 showcase we will only take photos or videos if every family agrees, and we will not share them online.

  • Projects stay yours. Your child’s project folder stays on the Mac it was made on. If you want a copy, we can put it on a USB stick for you.

AI chatbots at home: a few simple rules

Many families now use AI chatbots. If yours does, these rules help keep children safe:

  • Never type real names, addresses, school names, phone numbers, passwords or photos into a chatbot.

  • Check what it says. Chatbots can sound very sure and still be wrong. In week 9 your child builds a tiny “text generator” and sees why.

  • Say when you used it, just like you would credit a friend who helped.

  • Follow the service’s age rules (many are 13+), and use it together with your child.

How you can help

  • Ask your child to explain. You do not need to know any coding. “Show me what it does” and “How did you make it do that?” are the best questions there are.

  • Do not fix their code. When they are stuck, ask: “What did you expect? What happened instead? What could you try?” Getting unstuck is the skill we are building.

  • Celebrate bugs. Every programmer makes mistakes all day. Finding one is a win.

  • Protect 15–20 minutes for homework once a week, ideally soon after the session.

  • Mind screen time. A stretch break every 20 minutes and the 20-20-20 eye rule (every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds) help a lot.
