Design first

Builders draw plans before they pour concrete. Programmers design before they type. Your design answers three questions: Which classes do I need? What does each one know and do? Who works with whom?

CRC cards

A CRC card is an index card for one class: the Class name, its Responsibilities (what it knows and does) and its Collaborators (the classes it works with). Here is one from the reference project, Maze Muncher:

Tip: if a card’s responsibility list gets long, that class is doing too much – split it in two.

Class diagrams: “is a” and “has a”

A class diagram shows all the classes and how they connect. An arrow with a hollow triangle means is a (inheritance). A line with a diamond means has a (composition).

Read it aloud: a Game has a Maze, a Player and some Ghosts; a Chaser is a Ghost.

Milestones and save points

Big projects are built in small steps. After each step works, you commit – a save point you can always return to.

A good commit message starts with a verb and says what changed: Add Maze class that loads a grid, Fix player walking through walls. Your project needs 8 or more meaningful commits by the showcase.

SafetyMay I use this? Licenses

Everything in your project must be yours or allowed. Python’s standard library is free to use, and your own drawings and sounds are yours. For anything made by someone else, look at its license: CC0 = free for anyone; CC BY = free if you give credit; “All rights reserved” or no license = do not use. Never download anything without a parent, and list every borrowed item in the “Credits” part of your README.
